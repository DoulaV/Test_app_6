# Brand Architect

Given a creator's goal, audience, existing posts and background, produce a written brand definition (desired outcome, associations kept and refused, positioning gap, brand story in three parts, core topic, 80/20 split and expansion path), or audit an existing account's accidental brand and name the drift, and turn the result into operating rules the other skills in this repo can execute against.

Learned from: How to Build a Personal Brand (Full Course) (https://youtu.be/Ch4Sl0POBhU). The timestamped beat sheet is
in `references/source-notes.md`; anything copied from the screen is in
`references/verbatim-assets.md`.

## Where this sits among the other skills

This is the layer above all the others. `algorithm-strategist` starts at
"who is your avatar", which already assumes the creator knows who they are.
This skill answers that, and hands down four things the other skills consume:
the core topic (becomes the topic band), the association sentence (becomes
the fingerprint every post should reinforce), the refusal list (kills ideas
before they are made), and the brand story (becomes the raw material the
story skills shape).

Run order for a creator starting from nothing or from a mess:

1. `brand-architect` fixes who they are and what they are known for.
2. `algorithm-strategist` turns that into an avatar, a band, and a plan.
3. `brand-idea-miner` sources ideas inside the band.
4. `hook-writer` writes the opening, `story-loop-writer` the body.
5. `copy-sharpener` sharpens every word, caption and call to action.

Come back to this skill when the answer is about identity rather than
performance: an invitation to accept or refuse, a second topic, a bio, a
pinned video, the reason a large audience does not convert. Stay in
`algorithm-strategist` when the question is why a particular video died.

One rule crosses both and is resolved here rather than there: the 20%
off-band budget, in step 8 and in "The off-band budget" below.

## Prerequisites

- What the creator ultimately wants out of the audience (a business outcome, a career, a body of work, a cause), roughly who that audience is, and a sample of what they have already posted. Ask for the desired outcome if it is missing, because every other answer is derived backwards from it. If the creator says they do not know, run the audit path first and infer the outcome from what they keep returning to.
- Ingestion note: this source was read from YouTube captions only, so there is no visual layer. The on-screen worksheets are not captured; the frameworks themselves are fully spoken.

## Procedure

1. **State the working definition before any advice, because it changes what the task is.** Branding is a pairing of things. Good branding is an intentional pairing of relevant things done consistently. Brand is the byproduct: the association the audience inherently holds. Nike paired itself with Michael Jordan and owns athletic greatness in basketball; Harley-Davidson paired itself with freedom for so long that the customer pictures the open road. This reframing matters operationally, because it turns brand from a feeling into a repeatable action: decide the association, then pair yourself with it over and over. Say this to the user in one or two lines; it is the reason the rest of the procedure works. (see source-notes: t=01:05, t=01:30, t=03:00)
2. **Audit the brand that already exists before designing one.** The brand exists whether or not it was chosen; if the creator did not shape the associations, the audience did it for them. Most people arrive with an accidental brand, not a blank page. Read the last 15 to 30 posts and write two columns: what a stranger would say this account is about, and what the creator says it is about. Name every gap between them. Pull the recurring praise out of the comments, because that is the association that is actually landing, and pull the recurring objection, because that is the friction. An audit finding beats a designed intention every time, since it is evidence and the intention is a guess. (see source-notes: t=03:40, t=08:20, t=17:00, t=22:00)
3. **Run the Brand Journey Framework backwards, four questions in order.** What is the desired outcome; what do I have to be known for in order for that to happen; what do I have to do in order to be known for that; what do I have to learn in order to do that. Working backwards is the point: the fourth question lands on a day-one action, and the first question stops the most common failure, which is posting with no idea what it is meant to accomplish. Alice asks which road to take, is asked where she is trying to end up, says she does not know, and is told that either road will take her there. Write all four answers down as sentences, not bullets, and keep them where the creator can see them. (see source-notes: t=04:00, t=05:30, t=05:45)
4. **Write both association lists: what you want to be associated with, and what you refuse.** The first list most people can produce. The second is the one Caleb says nearly everyone ignores, and in a saturated market it is what makes you distinct, because you are defined by what you will not do as much as by what you will. The refusal list is also the decision rule for every collaboration, podcast, sponsor and shared stage, since a pairing transfers association in both directions: appearing repeatedly alongside people the audience distrusts makes the audience aware of you in light of that distrust, and awareness is not worth that trade. Aim for five to seven on each side, concrete enough to answer a real invitation with. When an invitation is genuinely borderline, Caleb's own tiebreak is the gut: if it gives you a bad feeling, it is not worth the exposure, whatever the audience size. (see source-notes: t=06:00, t=07:30, t=10:00, t=11:56)
5. **Position into a gap rather than into the middle of the field.** Find what is missing in the industry and own it. There are three places a gap can come from: the information you have that others do not, your philosophy on the subject, or your personality and the way you see it. Caleb's own worked example is positioning against a space obsessed with virality and view counts by standing for brands built on long-term trust. The fourth source is the creator's own story, which is where the genuinely unduplicable positions come from: the challenges, turning points and experiences nobody else had. Test the position by asking what a competitor would have to give up to copy it. (see source-notes: t=12:00, t=12:20, t=13:00, t=14:30)
6. **Write the Brand Story in three parts: Catalyst, Core Truth, Proof.** Offered as a replacement for problem-transformation-outcome, which is too narrow for a person. Catalyst is why the brand exists, because something needed to change: what needs to change, what do you see that others do not, why do you feel the need to act on it. Core Truth is what makes you different, because blending in is being forgotten: what makes you different, what does the audience already praise you for, why would they care and what does it give them. Proof is how the identity is reinforced every day, in what you say and in what you do, not once in a launch post. Show the truth rather than claiming it: instead of Jeff Bezos saying he is resourceful, the story of the door he used as a desk. Nail the three and the brand does not just have a story, it has loyalty. (see source-notes: t=26:30, t=27:00, t=28:30, t=30:30, t=16:20, t=33:00)
7. **Choose a narrow core topic, then set the 80/20 split deliberately.** The friend who is great at many things reads as good at none; the friend obsessed with woodworking reads as credible, so credibility comes from obsession. Gary Vaynerchuk spent years on Wine Library TV before marketing, and the wine is what bought the right to talk about marketing. But a single topic forever bores the audience and bores the creator, so spend roughly 80% on the core topic, ideally the one aligned with the offer or revenue, and roughly 20% on genuine other interests. The work-colleague analogy: one thing in common makes you colleagues, two makes you work friends, more makes you friends who stay friends after you leave. Each extra interest is another at-bat for a stranger to connect. Choose pillars by three criteria: what the creator is most convicted about, because conviction transfers; what resonates with the audience right now; and what they can explain in a way that makes action easy. (see source-notes: t=33:00, t=34:20, t=35:00, t=46:00, t=47:30, t=48:00, t=50:00)
8. **Price the 20% instead of spending it blindly, and say what it costs.** This is the one place the source contradicts algorithm-strategist, which says never go off-band because even a viral off-avatar video blends the fit score and costs the next several posts. Both frames are right about different systems: Kallaway is optimising distribution, which does punish topic drift, and Caleb is optimising the relationship, because a perfectly on-band account is a topic feed, which caps at useful and never reaches loyal. The resolution, which neither source states alone: off-band content is not free and it is not forbidden, it is priced. It costs reach on the following posts and buys depth of attachment. So spend it deliberately, spend it when there is reach to spare rather than while trying to break a plateau, keep it genuinely the creator's own rather than random, and connect it back to the core association wherever that is honest. An account still establishing its fit score sits near zero percent; an account with a reliable band and a loyalty problem should be spending the full 20%. (see source-notes: t=48:00, t=45:00)
9. **Plan the expansion path: credibility first, layers not leaps, and return to roots.** Do not speak on a subject before being excellent at it, and expand only once known for the first topic with consistent positive engagement, because that success is what buys credibility in the next area. Amazon started with books. Expand in layers, not leaps: every new topic should feel natural rather than abrupt, which in practice means the new topic shares an audience, a philosophy or a skill with the old one. Never lose sight of the core identity and where you started, and return to the roots on purpose: Gary still brings back Wine Library TV, Knocked Loose still play the original hardcore tracks live, and that is what lets day-one fans feel kept. Write the expansion as an ordered list with the trigger that unlocks each step, not as a wish list. (see source-notes: t=37:00, t=39:00, t=39:50, t=40:00, t=43:20)
10. **Convert the definition into operating rules, then hand off.** A brand document nobody executes is worth nothing, and the Proof part of the story is daily behaviour. End every run with: the one-line association sentence, the refusal list as a yes/no test for invitations, the pillar split as a posting ratio, three recurring objections to answer inside content, and the next expansion trigger. Then hand off: algorithm-strategist turns the core topic into an avatar and a band, brand-idea-miner sources ideas inside it, hook-writer and story-loop-writer write the piece, copy-sharpener sharpens the words. This skill decides who the creator is; those decide what gets posted. (see source-notes: t=30:30, t=51:00)

## Decision rules

- When the user asks what to post about, what their niche is, or why nothing is working, and has never written down a desired outcome: stop and run the Brand Journey backwards first; every downstream answer changes with the outcome (see source-notes: t=04:00, t=05:30)
- When the user already has an account with posts: audit before designing: read the posts and comments, write what a stranger would say it is about next to what the creator says, and work from the gap (see source-notes: t=03:40, t=22:00)
- When the user asks whether to accept a collaboration, podcast, sponsorship, guest spot or shared platform: check it against the refusal list and ask what association it transfers in both directions; if it conflicts, decline, because awareness is not worth the trade (see source-notes: t=10:00, t=11:56)
- When the user wants to broaden into a second topic: ask whether they are known for the first one yet with consistent positive engagement; if not, hold, and if yes, find the layer that connects the two rather than leaping (see source-notes: t=37:00, t=43:20)
- When the user is new, has no established reach, or is trying to break a plateau: put the split near 100/0 and say why: the 20% is paid for with reach they do not have yet (see source-notes: t=48:00)
- When the user has reliable reach but weak follower conversion or weak loyalty: spend the full 20% on genuine other interests, and say which ones, because loyalty comes from having more than one thing in common (see source-notes: t=50:00)
- When the user describes themselves with adjectives (authentic, resourceful, passionate): refuse the adjective and ask for the story that proves it; put the story in the brand instead (see source-notes: t=16:20)
- When the user wants to chase a trend or copy a format because it is working for someone else: check it against the associations and the position; a trend that reinforces the association is fine, one that only borrows attention is not (see source-notes: t=25:00)
- When audience feedback suggests changing the message itself: let feedback change how the idea is presented, never what the idea is; catering the substance to the audience is what makes a politician (see source-notes: t=26:00)
- When the brand definition is finished: hand off to algorithm-strategist for the avatar and band, brand-idea-miner for ideas, hook-writer and story-loop-writer for the script, copy-sharpener for the words (see source-notes: t=51:00)

## Known gotchas

- Treating intentional as the same as good. A creator can intentionally and consistently pair themselves with the wrong things, and the audience will form exactly that association. Fix: Check the pairing against the desired outcome, not just against consistency. (see source-notes: t=09:00, t=09:10)
- Only writing the associations you want and skipping the ones you refuse. Fix: The refusal list is the half that protects the brand and the only one that answers real invitations. Insist on it. (see source-notes: t=07:30)
- Sharing only wins. The friend who posts nothing but wins becomes unbelievable, and a stranger is more skeptical than a friend. Fix: Include the failures and the costs. Caleb's own first video covers a client halving his compensation and having to fire friends he had hired. (see source-notes: t=18:00)
- Answering objections only when asked. The audience forms objections silently and most never comment them. Fix: Mine the comment section for the recurring skepticism and answer it inside the content before it is raised. (see source-notes: t=17:00)
- Reading the 80/20 split as permission to post anything. Fix: The 20% is the creator's real other interests, deliberately chosen and paid for in reach, not a random feed. (see source-notes: t=48:00, t=45:00)
- Applying the 80/20 split to an account with no established fit score. Fix: This is an inference the source does not make. The split assumes an audience that can already find you. Start near zero and earn the room. (see source-notes: t=48:00)
- Turning the guidelines into rules and refusing anything off-pillar forever. Fix: Caleb is explicit that these are guidelines. The Harley video is not a pillar and he makes it anyway, because it rounds out the brand and makes him interesting. (see source-notes: t=44:00, t=45:00)
- Treating the brand document as the deliverable. Fix: Proof is daily behaviour. End with operating rules and a handoff, not a manifesto. (see source-notes: t=30:30)

## Output format

Requests come in three shapes and only the first uses the whole format.
Decide which one you are in before writing anything.

**Design** ("what should I be known for", "help me define my brand"). Use the
full format below.

**Audit** ("is my account working", "why does nobody follow"). Lead with the
audit table and the drift verdict, then give only the parts of the format the
audit showed were missing or wrong. Do not regenerate a whole brand when the
finding is that two associations are fighting each other.

**Decision** (one invitation, one sponsorship, one new topic, one bio). Answer
the decision first, in the first two sentences, with the reasoning from the
association lists. Then give only the piece of the format that stops the
question recurring: usually the refusal list, sometimes the pillar split. Do
not produce a brand document because someone asked whether to go on a podcast.

**When the prerequisites are missing.** The desired outcome is required and it
is often absent. Never hold the whole answer hostage to it. Instead: answer
everything that does not depend on it, state the two or three plausible
outcomes and which part of the answer each one would change, ask the one
question, and mark the affected sections provisional. A decision request can
almost always be answered without the outcome, because a refusal is a refusal
under any outcome. A design request cannot, so deliver the audit and the
association lists and leave the Brand Journey table for the reply.

Open the design output with the single association sentence, then the
supporting work. Never open with a preamble about brand being important.

**Association sentence.** One line, in the form "<name> is the person who
<does the specific thing> for <the people>, and stands for <the belief>".
This is the whole deliverable compressed. Everything else justifies it.

**Brand Journey table** (always, when designing):

```
| Question | Answer |
| Desired outcome | |
| Known for | |
| Must do | |
| Must learn (start here) |  |
```

The fourth row must contain an action possible this week. If it does not,
the answers above it are still abstractions.

**Associations, both lists:**

```
| Want to be associated with | Refuse to be associated with |
```

Five to seven a side. Each refusal must be concrete enough to decide a real
invitation with. "Bad content" is not; "engagement-bait stitches and
reaction videos" is.

**Position.** Two or three sentences: what the field currently sounds like,
the gap, which of the four sources it comes from (information, philosophy,
personality, story), and the test result, meaning what a competitor would
have to give up to copy it.

**Brand story.** Three short paragraphs headed Catalyst, Core Truth, Proof.
Write them in the creator's voice, first person, as prose they could read
aloud. The Proof paragraph must name behaviours and recurring content, not
adjectives.

**Pillars and split.** A list with a percentage against each, summing to a
hundred, plus one line saying what the off-band share is costing and whether
the account can currently afford it.

**Expansion path.** An ordered list of topics with the trigger that unlocks
each one, and one line on how each connects back to the core.

**Operating rules.** Five to seven imperative lines the creator can pin
somewhere: the association sentence, the yes/no invitation test, the posting
ratio, the recurring objections to answer inside content, and the next
expansion trigger.

**Hand-off.** One closing line naming the next skill.

## Auditing a brand that already exists (not shown in the video)

The course is written for someone starting from zero. Almost nobody is. An
existing account already has a brand; it was just assembled by the audience
out of whatever was posted. Audit it before designing anything, because the
audit is evidence and the design is a guess.

Read the last 15 to 30 posts plus the comments and fill this in:

```
| Post | Topic | Association it reinforces | Off the core? | What the comments say back |
```

Then write the three findings:

**Stranger's summary.** In one sentence, what someone who scrolled this
profile for thirty seconds would say this account is about. Write it
unkindly. If the honest version is "posts videos", say that.

**Owner's summary.** The same sentence as the creator would give it.

**Drift.** The gap between them, named specifically. The four common shapes:
*diffuse* (no repeated association at all), *mismatched* (a clear
association, but not the one the creator wants), *borrowed* (the association
belongs to a format or a trend rather than to the person), and *stranded*
(the association is right but nothing in the content tells the viewer there
is more of it, so nobody follows).

The recurring praise in the comments is the association that is actually
landing, and it usually beats the one the creator intended, so treat it as
the strongest input to the Core Truth. The recurring objection is the
friction, and it belongs inside future content before it is raised.

## The off-band budget (not shown in the video)

Step 8 states the resolution. This is the table for applying it.

```
| Account state                                  | Off-band share | Why |
| No established fit score, under ~10 posts      | 0%             | Nothing to spend; the algorithm has no read on you yet |
| Reach inconsistent, or trying to break a floor | 0 to 5%        | Every post is buying fingerprint clarity |
| Reliable reach, weak follow or comment rate    | 15 to 20%      | The reach exists and is not converting; loyalty is the missing piece |
| Reliable reach, strong loyalty                 | 20%            | Maintenance; keeps the creator from burning out on one topic |
```

Three conditions on anything spent from the budget. It must be genuinely the
creator's own interest, not a topic picked because it performs. It must be
connected back to the core association wherever that is honest, which
usually costs one sentence. And it should be posted when the previous post
performed well, not after a flop, because it spends reach rather than
earning it.

If the creator cannot name a real second interest, the correct split is
100/0 and the honest answer is that the brand has a personality problem, not
a topic problem.

## Vulnerability without oversharing (not shown in the video)

The course says share the failures, not only the wins, and it is right about
why: a person who only posts wins is unbelievable, and a stranger is more
skeptical than a friend. It does not say where the line is.

The working test: share the failure you have finished having. A failure with
a conclusion is a story and it builds trust. A failure still in progress is
a request for support, and it moves the audience from viewer to caretaker,
which is not a brand.

Two more boundaries. The failure should be the creator's own, not someone
else's told without their consent. And it should cost something to tell; a
"failure" that flatters (working too hard, caring too much) reads as a
humblebrag and does more damage than posting nothing.

## Building a brand outside English (not shown in the video)

Nothing in the source addresses non-English or non-Western audiences, and
two things do not transfer cleanly.

**The vulnerability norm differs.** In audiences where public admission of
failure reads as complaint rather than as candour, the trust that Caleb gets
from confession has to be bought another way: through specificity and through
cost. Naming an exact number, an exact date, a decision that was expensive,
does the same work without the register of confession. Test a vulnerable
line by asking whether the creator's own audience would read it as honest or
as asking for sympathy, and cut anything in the second group.

**The association set is local.** The reference points that carry meaning
(brands, figures, places, phrases) are not the ones in the course, and a
position defined against a Western industry conversation will not read as a
position at all. Build the "what does the field currently sound like" step
from accounts the creator's own audience actually watches, in their own
language.

The rest transfers unchanged. Pairing, refusal, gap positioning, catalyst,
core truth, proof, narrowness before expansion: none of these depend on
language.

## Worked example

Input: a structural engineer, eight years in, posting occasional building
photos to about 900 followers. Wants to end up consulting independently
rather than working for a firm. No idea what to post.

Audit: stranger's summary is "an engineer who posts nice photos of
buildings". Owner's summary is "I explain how buildings work". Drift is
*diffuse*, because the photos carry no repeated association. The one post
that outperformed everything was a thread on why a car park collapsed, and
the comments on it were all versions of "why was this allowed".

Brand Journey, backwards: outcome is independent consulting clients, mostly
developers and architects. Known for: the person who can tell you what will
fail before it fails. Must do: publish a failure breakdown every week, using
public reports, in language a developer understands. Must learn: how to read
a structural failure report fast, and how to explain load paths without
jargon. The last one starts Monday.

Associations kept: building failure, plain explanation, independence from
any contractor, historical cases, safety over cost. Refused: contractor
sponsorships, blaming named individuals, speculating before a report is
published, anything that reads as disaster entertainment.

Position: the field is split between academic papers nobody reads and news
coverage with no engineering in it. The gap is information, held by someone
who can explain it. A competitor would have to give up contractor sponsorship
money to copy it, which most will not.

Brand story. Catalyst: buildings keep failing the same way and the knowledge
that would have stopped it sits in reports nobody reads. Core Truth: the
audience already praises the plain explanation, so the difference is
translation, not expertise. Proof: a failure breakdown every week, always
sourced to a published report, never sponsored by anyone who builds.

Pillars: 80% failure breakdowns, 20% the restoration of an old motorbike,
which is genuinely his and quietly reinforces "things are built to last or
they are not". Reach is inconsistent, so the split starts at 100/0 and the
motorbike waits until the breakdowns land reliably.

Expansion path: failure breakdowns, then how to commission an inspection
(unlocks when breakdowns hold engagement for two months), then materials and
cost trade-offs (unlocks when developers start commenting rather than
engineers).

Hand-off: `algorithm-strategist` to turn "building failure breakdowns for
developers and architects" into an avatar and a posting plan.
