# Brand Idea Miner

Given a niche and target viewer, build the research setup (watchlist, thresholds), mine outliers into seven-lego breakdowns with a daily and long-term log, and turn them into a shortlist of remixed ideas that each pass the five obsession elements, with the hold and remix decisions and the proof plan stated.

Learned from: 2 videos ([V1] How to Build an Audience That's Obsessed With You (The Power of Suggestion); [V2] The Science-Based Method To Create Irresistible Ideas). The timestamped beat sheet is
in `references/source-notes.md`; anything copied from the screen is in
`references/verbatim-assets.md`.

## Related skills

Run `algorithm-strategist` first when the avatar and topic band are not yet
fixed; this skill mines ideas inside that band. Hand the top idea to
`hook-writer` for the opening lines and `story-loop-writer` for the body. The
seven legos map onto them: the hook lego is hook-writer's job, the story
structure lego is story-loop-writer's, and this skill decides topic, angle,
visual format, key visuals, and audio, plus the proof plan.

## Prerequisites

- The niche, the one specific viewer (from algorithm-strategist's avatar statement when it exists), the platform, and either a list of accounts the viewer already watches or permission to build one. Access to a research tool such as Sandcastles is optional; the manual path is documented.

## Procedure

1. **Treat every idea as seven legos and never as a topic: topic, angle, hook (spoken, text, visual), story structure, visual format, key visuals, audio.** Flops happen because all seven were guessed at once. Separating them lets you borrow what is proven and invent only where invention pays. Two videos can share a topic and be completely different videos because the angle differs. (see source-notes: V2 t=00:48, V2 t=01:24, V2 t=04:49)
2. **Build the watchlist: 20 to 30 accounts in the core niche that already perform, plus separate lists for adjacent niches.** Outliers hide in the noise of a feed; a curated list is the only way to see them. Seed it four ways: an existing list, the accounts a known-great creator follows, platform search by topic, or a tool database. Ten to fifteen minutes. (see source-notes: V2 t=12:50, V2 t=13:43, V2 t=14:04, V2 t=14:21)
3. **Pull outliers only from the same content type (short vertical video on Reels, TikTok, Shorts), scored as views over the channel's typical views, filtered to 5x and above with engagement rate 0.5% and above, in a date window matched to the content (7 to 14 days for news, 3 to 12 months for evergreen), sorted by outlier score descending.** Channel-relative scoring makes small and large accounts comparable; a 50x on a small account means something happened. Same content type matters because consumption psychology is type-specific: a tweet's hook, structure, and format do not carry over to a reel, so cross-type sources give only topic and angle and leave you guessing the other five. The engagement floor screens out paid posts with bought reach. (see source-notes: V2 t=03:05, V2 t=03:27, V2 t=09:51, V2 t=10:33, V2 t=16:17)
4. **Analyze each outlier across the seven legos after a first 'is this interesting and relevant to my viewer' filter, and log in two layers: daily inspiration per topic (topic, angle, hook notes, key visuals, link, audio notes, what worked, what is weak) and long-term patterns (hook structures, story structures, visual formats) that only show across many videos.** Daily entries feed next week's videos; patterns across weeks tell you which legos your niche has already solved. Twenty to thirty videos in a session yields a market-validated shortlist. Always note the weak point: that is where your version beats the original. (see source-notes: V2 t=17:59, V2 t=18:41, V2 t=19:28, V2 t=20:16, V2 t=20:25)
5. **For each shortlisted idea, decide hold and remix: hold the legos the pattern across winners has proven (usually hook style and visual format, sometimes topic), remix the rest, and make the angle non-obvious by colliding the topic with a framework, fact, or story from outside the niche.** This is where the two sources meet. Holding proven structure de-risks the rep (V2). But recycling the niche's angle with better editing never builds an obsessed audience; creativity is two worlds colliding, so the angle must be new (V1). Structure from outliers, idea from collision. (see source-notes: V2 t=06:08, V2 t=06:32, V2 t=20:43, V1 t=05:59, V1 t=06:11, V1 t=06:34, V1 t=20:34)
6. **Pass every idea through the five obsession elements before it is made: relevant and relatable to the one viewer; small effort for a big reward; non-obvious; shown with proof; actionable in steps.** The five elements are how the power of suggestion works across all three levels of obsession: value (signal), a reframed belief (contrast, relatable character, small cost), and a result the viewer gets by copying you (quantitative proof, atomic steps). Each has a named failure and fix: too broad, so narrow until personal; overcomplicated to seem valuable, so simplify until anyone can act; same ideas as everyone, so combine from outside the niche; ideas without evidence, so get results first; theory without execution, so break into steps. (see source-notes: V1 t=18:34, V1 t=18:50, V1 t=19:26, V1 t=20:10, V1 t=20:57, V1 t=21:24)
7. **Write the proof plan for each idea: the specific number or example that validates the claim, and whether it is the creator's own result or borrowed and cited. Never let the script state the creator's superiority; state results and method and let the viewer conclude it.** Without proof it is opinion; with proof it is a source of truth. Authority claimed directly raises the viewer's guard; results stated plainly let them conclude authority themselves, which is where fandom comes from. 'This exact strategy grew someone from 2 to 15,000 followers in 6 weeks' beats 'this will help you grow faster'. (see source-notes: V1 t=01:07, V1 t=01:54, V1 t=07:42, V1 t=15:28, V1 t=21:06, V1 t=21:17)
8. **Deliver a ranked shortlist with each idea's seven legos, its hold and remix decisions, its five-element check, its proof plan, and the outlier it beats and how; then hand the top idea to hook-writer and story-loop-writer.** The shortlist is the bridge from research to production. Ranking by five-element pass plus outlier strength keeps the creator making validated, non-obvious, provable videos instead of the loudest one. (see source-notes: V2 t=20:25, V1 t=21:42)

## Decision rules

- When the user has no research tool: run the manual path: a spreadsheet of watchlist accounts with each account's median views over its last 10 to 20 posts, then per-video ratio; check a handful of accounts a day rather than all at once (see source-notes: V2 t=03:13, V2 t=12:06)
- When an outlier is from a different content type (a tweet, a blog, a long YouTube video): take only its topic and angle, and rebuild the other five legos from your niche's proven patterns; flag the rep as higher risk (see source-notes: V2 t=10:26, V2 t=10:41)
- When a remix keeps the outlier's angle: reject it as recycling; the angle must come from a collision with something outside the niche (see source-notes: V1 t=05:59, V1 t=06:15, V2 t=06:45)
- When the creator has no results of their own yet: use borrowed proof (a named case study, cited data, a documented example) and say it is borrowed; plan the first own-result video as a Level 3 piece once a result exists (see source-notes: V1 t=15:12, V1 t=21:17)
- When an idea looks viral but fails relevance to the one viewer: drop it; broad topics chase views and kill obsession, and they muddy the account's fit (see algorithm-strategist) (see source-notes: V1 t=19:04, V1 t=19:13)
- When the idea's path to the result looks like years of effort: reframe to the smallest input that produces a visible reward, or pick a different idea; complexity kills action (see source-notes: V1 t=11:22, V1 t=19:44)
- When the draft tells the viewer what to do or claims the creator is the best: rewrite as proof plus the exact steps the creator took, and let the viewer conclude (see source-notes: V1 t=14:05, V1 t=14:29, V1 t=17:43)
- When content is news-driven: use a 7 to 14 day window and check daily; evergreen content can use 3 to 12 months (see source-notes: V2 t=16:32)

## Known gotchas

- Treating an idea as a topic and guessing the other six legos. Fix: Break it into seven and borrow the proven ones. (see source-notes: V2 t=00:56, V2 t=05:01)
- Sorting by raw views, which favors big channels. Fix: Use views over channel average; a 50x on a small channel is the signal. (see source-notes: V2 t=03:27)
- Counting boosted brand-deal posts as outliers. Fix: Engagement rate floor of 0.5%. (see source-notes: V2 t=16:25)
- Remixing with better editing but the same angle as the niche. Fix: Collide with an outside framework; non-obvious ideas create obsessive audiences. (see source-notes: V1 t=05:59, V1 t=20:18)
- Claiming authority or commanding action. Fix: Show the result and the exact inputs; suggestion is the viewer concluding on their own. (see source-notes: V1 t=01:17, V1 t=14:20)
- Overcomplicating the solution to seem valuable. Fix: Simplify until anyone can act. (see source-notes: V1 t=19:48, V1 t=20:02)
- Expecting structural patterns to appear in one day's batch. Fix: Track hook, story, and format patterns across weeks; log daily inspiration separately. (see source-notes: V2 t=18:25)

## Output format

Open with what the user gets in one or two sentences, then the blocks that
apply:

**Research setup.** The watchlist (or how to build it in 15 minutes), the
filter preset, and the date window chosen for this niche, with the manual
path if there is no tool.

**Outlier log.** For each outlier analyzed:

```
| Outlier (link or description) | Score | Topic | Angle | Hook (spoken / text / visual) | Story structure | Visual format | Key visuals | Audio | What worked | Weak point |
```

**Shortlist.** Three to five ideas, ranked. For each:

```
### Idea N: <title>
Beats: <which outlier, and the weak point it fixes>
Hold: <legos kept, and the pattern across winners that justifies each>
Remix: <legos changed>
Collision: <the outside-niche framework, fact, or story that makes the angle non-obvious>
Seven legos: topic / angle / hook (spoken, text, visual) / story structure / visual format / key visuals / audio
Five elements: relevant+relatable [yes/partly/no, why] | small effort big reward [..] | non-obvious [..] | proof [..] | actionable [..]
Proof plan: <the specific number or example, own or borrowed and cited>
Level reached: 1 signal / 2 belief / 3 action, and what would lift it a level
```

**Audit** (when the user brings an idea or draft): the five-element row, the
level it reaches, the bank-shot check (does it claim authority or command
action anywhere), and the rewrite of the failing elements. Write the primary
rewrite in the full Idea template above so the seven legos and the proof plan
are forced; alternates can be shorter. An idea that fails signal outright
(no value the viewer would thank you for) reaches "below level 1"; say so
rather than rounding up.

Include the Research setup and Outlier log blocks only when the user has a
watchlist, a tool, or pasted references. Otherwise say which legos are
therefore guessed and give the 15-minute seed path instead of inventing an
outlier log.

**Hand-off.** One line: which idea goes to hook-writer and story-loop-writer
next.

## Manual research path (not shown in the videos)

Without a tool: a spreadsheet with one row per watchlist account. For each,
record the view counts of its last 10 to 20 reels and take the median (the
median resists the outliers you are looking for). Then, for any video that
catches your eye, divide its views by that account's median. Anything at 5x
or above with visible engagement (likes plus comments over views at 0.5% or
above) goes into the outlier log. Check five to eight accounts a day rather
than all thirty at once; the long-term pattern log only fills across days
anyway.

## Generating the collision (not shown in the videos)

V1 says creativity is two worlds colliding and V2 says the angle is what you
remix. To produce candidates, pair the topic with three sources from outside
the niche and keep the one that explains the topic better than the niche's
own framing: a psychology or behavior principle; a sport, cooking, or craft
process; a money or engineering analogy; a historical or cultural reference
the viewer already holds an opinion about (this also serves as the
cult-hopping anchor from hook-writer). The test: could a competitor in the
niche have said this without leaving the niche? If yes, it is not a
collision.

## Minimal idea development (not shown in the videos)

V2 defers reassembly to a later video. The minimum is: hook style and visual
format come from the long-term pattern log (the held legos), the angle comes
from the collision, the key visuals come from the proof plan (show the
number, the before and after, the exact input), and the story structure is
chosen by the level you are aiming at: a breakdown for level 1, a before and
after transformation for level 2, a step-by-step with results for level 3.

## Proof when you have no results yet (not shown in the videos)

V1's "get results first" assumes results exist. Until they do, use borrowed
proof: a named case study, a cited figure, or a documented example, said
plainly as borrowed ("a client of X grew from...", "the study found..."). Never
present borrowed results as your own, and never round a number up. Plan the
first own-result video as a level 3 piece the moment a real result exists,
since that is where the deepest loyalty forms.

## Honesty rule (not shown in the videos)

The presenter's vocabulary ("implant", "mind control") describes a real
mechanism: people trust conclusions they reach themselves. Use the mechanism
and drop the vocabulary. In practice: claim only what the proof supports,
show the inputs that produced the outputs, attribute borrowed proof, and let
the viewer decide what that says about you. Suggestion done honestly is
just showing your work.

## When a pattern has decayed (not shown in the videos)

V2 says winning combinations change over time. Treat a held lego as expired
when the last two weeks of outliers in the watchlist no longer share it;
re-open it for remix and hold whatever the new winners share instead.

## Worked example

The bank shot from V1 t=01:07, applied to an idea title: "Why we're the best
wedding photographers in Cairo: our 5 rules" claims authority and fails the
check; a competitor could post it word for word, so it fails the collision
test too. "The 5 rules that let us shoot a 400-guest wedding with one
photographer and deliver in 48 hours" states a result and a method, is
relevant to couples comparing vendors, non-obvious if the rules are (the
collision here is logistics from event catering, where one cook plates for
400), proven by the numbers, and actionable enough that a couple can ask
any photographer the same five questions. The viewer draws the conclusion the
first title tried to assert.
