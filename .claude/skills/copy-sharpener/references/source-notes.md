## Structure

- Opens with proof before promise: revenue dashboard, record, books, inside 45 seconds, then the syllabus.
- Holds attention with a cut every three to eight seconds, a named test per concept (firefighter, so what, deaf and mute, squint), and two full teardowns of real ads.
- Turns at 02:37 (writing is thinking), 19:49 (thinking to craft), 27:38 (craft to structure), 32:22 (skill to money).
- Closes on practice and persistence rather than tactics.

## Procedure (as taught)

1. Before writing, run the 6Ps: people, positioning, promise, proof, priority, process. (t=03:30)
2. Build an empathy map for the avatar: current daily reality, current frustrations, future fears, future goals. Do not write until you have an overabundance of material. (t=05:02, t=06:10)
3. Mine your audience's actual words from recorded calls, comments, or DMs, and say those exact words back as hooks. (t=06:20)
4. Find the true positioning by naming the process nobody else bothers to state. (t=08:46)
5. Convert every feature into a core outcome with the So What ladder. (t=10:49)
6. Attach proof, matched to the avatar, that survives the deaf and mute test. (t=12:00)
7. Give a reason to act now: scarcity, deadline, or cost of inaction. (t=13:54)
8. Then write, and sharpen every line with the 5Cs: clear, concise, concrete, conversational, cadence. (t=19:49 to 27:38)
9. Score the hook with Benefit x Relevance x Credibility / Perceived effort. (t=27:38)
10. Structure the body with What/Why/How for teaching or But/Therefore for story. (t=29:18)
11. Close with one CTA: who, what, when, how. One CTA, one avatar, one offer. (t=30:50)

## Inferences and gaps

- (gap) No frames read; on-screen framework wording is Gemini's reading, though every framework is also spoken in the captions, so confidence is high.
- (gap) Everything assumes English. Hemingway does not support Arabic, cadence works differently in Arabic prose, and the fifth-grade reading level heuristic has no direct equivalent. A non-English adaptation is needed.
- (gap) The whole course assumes something is being sold. Emotional, artistic, or personal content has no offer, and a Who/What/When/How CTA can break the spell. Needs a rule for when to soften or drop the CTA.
- (gap) "The pain is the pitch" and mining people's exact words can slide into manipulation. The course gives no ethical boundary. One is required, consistent with the other skills in this repo.
- (gap) The exact-quote protocol assumes recorded sales calls. Creators without them need the equivalent source named: comments, DMs, replies, voice notes.
- (inference) The 5Cs are the genuinely portable part of the course. The 6Ps overlap heavily with audience work the other skills already do; the 5Cs and the CTA structure have no counterpart anywhere in this repo.

## Three highest-signal observations

1. The research is the work, and the highest-leverage version of it is repeating your audience's own recorded words back to them, because describing someone's pain better than they can implies you have the cure. (t=05:02 to 08:46)
2. Five sentence-level tests turn ordinary writing into copy: remove what is unclear rather than adding clarity, price every word at ten dollars, zoom in until it is visualizable, match the reader's spoken register, and vary sentence length so it reads like music. (t=19:49 to 27:38)
3. A hook can be scored rather than guessed, with benefit, relevance and credibility over perceived effort, and a close can be structured rather than improvised, with one action for one person by one deadline. (t=27:38, t=30:50)
