## framework (6Ps of copy thinking) (t=03:30)

```
People: who is the offer for.
Positioning: why choose you over anyone else.
Promise: what they get, as an outcome.
Proof: why they should trust you.
Priority: why act now.
Process: how the offer actually works.
```

## tool (empathy map) (t=05:02, t=06:10)

```
Draw two lines, one up and one down.
Current reality: what are they doing, seeing, hearing, smelling, feeling on a daily basis.
Current frustrations: what they dislike in daily life.
Future fears (hell): where they do not want to end up.
Future goals (heaven): the reality they want to reach.
Rule: do not start writing until you have an overabundance of material.
```

## principle (t=05:25, t=08:40, t=10:40)

```
Treat other people the way they want to be treated. It's putting yourself in their shoes, not them in yours.
If you can describe someone's painpoint better than they can, they'll subconsciously believe that you have the solution. The pain is the pitch.
The game is won before the pen is even used.
```

## tool (proof) (t=12:00, t=12:45, t=13:15)

```
Three types, weakest to strongest: authority by association, your own results, your client's results.
Trust is belief with evidence. Belief without evidence is called faith.
Deaf and mute test: you cannot speak, you can only point. You can't point at 'trusted by industry leaders', but you can point at the logos. You can't point at 'proven results', but you can point at the number. You can't point at 'highly experienced', but you can point at 400 clients, 11 years.
Proof is relative: match the case study to the avatar.
```

## tool (priority) (t=13:54, t=14:05, t=14:25)

```
Three levers: scarcity (limited spots), urgency (this goes away in X days), and cost of inaction (what happens if you stay on your current path).
People will jog towards pleasure but they will sprint away from pain.
Deprivation is the cousin of motivation.
```

## framework (5Cs of writing) (t=19:49, t=21:00, t=22:20, t=23:20, t=24:20, t=25:20)

```
Clear: they cannot buy if they do not understand. Do not write more clearly, remove what is unclear, from the end forwards.
Concise: every word costs $10. Ask what can be cut without removing quality or understanding.
Concrete: make it visualizable. Zoom in on the abstract until it can be pictured.
Conversational: match the reader's in-group language. Read it out loud.
Cadence: vary short, medium and long sentences. Write music, not words.
```

## passage (cadence, Gary Provost) (t=25:35)

```
This sentence has five words. Here are five more words. Five-word sentences are fine, but several together become monotonous. Listen to what is happening. The writing is getting boring. The sound of it drones. It's like a stuck record. The ear demands some variety.
Now listen. I vary the sentence length and I create music. Music. The writing sings. It has a pleasant rhythm, a lilt, a harmony. I use short sentences. And I use sentences of medium length. And sometimes, when I am certain the reader is rested, I will engage him with a sentence of considerable length, a sentence that burns with energy and builds with all the impetus of a crescendo, the roll of the drums, the crash of the cymbals, sounds that say, listen to this, it is important.
So write with a combination of short, medium, and long sentences. Create a sound that pleases the reader's ear. Don't just write words. Write music.
```

## tests (cadence) (t=26:35)

```
1. Read it out loud. Anywhere you stumble or it sounds unclear, rewrite it.
2. Squint your eyes and look only at the shape of the lines. Does the length vary, or is it one straight block?
```

## formula (hook score) (t=28:05)

```
Virality = Benefit x Relevance x Credibility / Perceived effort.
Benefit: why they should care.
Relevance: how many people this applies to.
Credibility: why they should listen to you.
Perceived effort: how much work it takes to read it and to act on it.
```

## example (scored hook) (t=28:35)

```
"I was employee number 30 at Facebook. Then I got fired. For a long time, I was bitter that they let me go. But the lessons I learned from Mark and Facebook helped me build a $100 million a year company. 10 lessons I learned from working directly under Mark Zuckerberg."
Credibility: employee 30 at Facebook. Benefit: the 10 lessons. Relevance: lessons apply to almost anyone. Perceived effort: near zero, and access to Zuckerberg is otherwise impossible.
```

## framework (body) (t=29:25, t=30:10)

```
Educational: What (the point) / Why (why they should care) / How (how to implement).
Story: But / Therefore. Linear is 'and then, and then'. Tension is 'I went to the grocery store, but I forgot my keys. Therefore I went home, but someone was in my kitchen.'
```

## framework (CTA) (t=30:50, t=31:25)

```
Who: name exactly who this is for.
What: the single action.
When: the deadline.
How: the mechanism.
One CTA to one avatar to one offer.
```

## principle (purpose of craft) (t=20:35, t=27:10)

```
It's not about how smart you are as the writer. It's about how smart the reader feels.
The best copywriting is like the windows in a mall. It's see-through. You're not trying to see the window, you're trying to see the stuff in the store.
```

## practice (t=22:40, t=31:50)

```
Keep a swipe file of every hook that caught you, then write down why it caught you.
Do copywork: hand-copy great writing to feel its rhythm.
Write 10+ short posts a day; a character limit forces concision and becomes the testing ground for hooks that later become ads and scripts.
```
