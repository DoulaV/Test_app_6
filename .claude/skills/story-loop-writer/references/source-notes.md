## Structure

- Opens: the formula promise plus a reframing (storytelling is programmable), then credentials.
- Holds attention: one metaphor carried all the way through (casino, blackjack), then the same story told twice so the viewer feels the difference on themselves, then tactics with paired good/bad script lines.
- Turns: 01:00 (neuroscience), 07:46 (live experiment), 13:26 (tactics), 20:39 (close). Each turn is itself a re-hook ("now the million-dollar question is...").
- Closes: recap as a four-word formula, mission statement, call to action.

## Procedure (as taught)

1. Before the story starts, establish stakes: a character to root for, something at risk, urgency. Check that the viewer knows what is at risk, who it is happening to, why it matters now. (t=02:37, t=14:30 to 16:15)
2. Load a big question early: give just enough specific context that the viewer forms a prediction, never a vague teaser. Before every important reveal, back up and add context. (t=03:05, t=16:16 to 17:50)
3. Build the head fake in two steps: work out what the viewer will naturally predict, then deliver a reveal that contradicts it yet is logical in hindsight, with clues planted earlier. (t=04:00, t=10:20, t=17:51 to 19:00)
4. Re-hook at every loop close: open the next question in the same breath, using bridge phrases; shrink the gap between hits. (t=05:05, t=11:41, t=19:00 to 20:39)
5. Repeat the loop for the length of the piece; the loop is medium-agnostic (videos, shorts, emails, tweets, sales calls). (t=06:05, t=14:15)

## Inferences and gaps

- (gap) No frames read; visual descriptions are Gemini's. The template database text is Gemini's reading of a document shown briefly on screen and may be partial.
- (inference) The video presents the loop as fractal: whole piece, each section, each paragraph. The presenter says "over and over" and "not every moment needs a big question, but every great story has a massive one towards the beginning", so the skill should distinguish the master loop from local loops.
- (gap) The neuroscience claims (prediction error yields the largest dopamine spike) are stated without sources. The skill should use them as a working model, not as medical fact.
- (gap) No method for auditing an existing script; the tactics section implies one (find loop closes, check for vague teasers, check stakes present) but does not state it as a checklist.
- (gap) No guidance on how many loops per length of content, or how to end a piece without a dangling re-hook.

## Three highest-signal observations

1. Attention is anticipation, not payoff: the viewer stays while an unresolved prediction is running, so the writer's job is to keep one open at all times. (t=01:10 to 02:30, t=05:37)
2. The loop is four moves in a fixed order, stakes, big question, head fake, re-hook, and the same flat story becomes gripping with no new facts, only these moves added. (t=02:37 to 06:05, t=07:46 to 12:30)
3. Two quality rules do most of the work: a big question must be specific enough to predict against (no empty teasers), and a head fake must be surprising yet logical in hindsight with clues planted (no random twists). (t=16:16 to 19:00)
