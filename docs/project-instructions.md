## Who I am

I am Akadoola. I make cinematic short films in Egyptian Arabic about men who
cannot talk about what they feel: emotional suppression, inherited silence,
and what it costs. Instagram @Akadoola, around 13,000 followers. Reels are my
only format so far.

My best video did 450,000 views and 18,000 likes.

## What I want you to know about my numbers

From the insights on that video, and this is the diagnosis to work from
rather than re-derive every time:

- 138,018 unique viewers, 1,444 new follows. That is 1.05%, which is mediocre.
- 3.27 views per viewer. People rewatch, and rewatch is what drove the reach.
- Average watch about 14 seconds on a 45 second film. Most viewers never reach
  the payoff.
- 187 comments against roughly 7,200 saves and sends.

The pattern: people treat my films as objects to keep and forward, not as a
person to talk to. The reach engine works. The relationship does not. My drift
shape is **stranded**: the association is right, but nothing in a film tells a
viewer there is more of this, so they finish a complete thing and leave.

Two consequences you should apply without being asked:

1. Every script needs a reason to expect the next one. A series name, a
   recurring opening, an unresolved thread, a stated cadence.
2. The payoff cannot sit at second 40. Something has to land before 14.

## What I am building

My name is Ahmed. The account is @Akadoola.

**Association sentence.** Ahmed is the person who turns Egyptian men's silence
into cinema, for the men who live inside that silence and the filmmakers who
want to shoot like that, and who stands for the belief that the feeling comes
first and the craft exists to serve it.

**The structural fact that governs everything below.** The audience my films
attract and the audience that will buy my products are not the same people. A
man who recognises his father in a 45 second film is moved. He does not buy a
powergrade. The people who buy LUTs, powergrades, SFX packs and courses are
filmmakers and creators. Both audiences are real and I want both, so every
decision below is about the bridge between them rather than about choosing
one.

**The bridge is the craft, made visible.** The films are one asset with two
faces. The front is the feeling, and that is what reaches the 400 million. The
back is how it was shot, graded and mixed, and that is what sells. Same asset,
two angles. This is why I do not need a second account and must not start one.

### Position

The field around me is three things. Faceless cinematic edit accounts with
borrowed audio and no author. Filmmaking tutorial accounts, mostly in English
or MSA, teaching technique with no body of work behind it. And psychologists
and men's-development accounts talking about male emotion with no images at
all.

The gap is someone who makes genuinely moving Egyptian films, shows the craft
behind them, and is a visible person doing both. Nobody is standing there.

Sources: information (my actual grade and workflow), personality, and story.
The copy test: a tutorial account would have to make films that actually move
people. An edit account would have to put a face and an original script to it.
A psychologist would have to learn to shoot. Almost nobody will pay those
prices, which is what makes the position defensible.

### Pillars and split

- **85% the films.** They are the work, the reach and the portfolio at once.
- **15% the craft, made visible inside the film posts** rather than as
  separate posts: a second slide, the grade in the caption, the setup in a
  story. This costs no reach because it is not a topic change.
- **0% off-band personal interests, for now.** One 450K outlier against 13K
  followers is inconsistent reach, not reliable reach, so the off-band budget
  is not affordable yet. Revisit when three of five consecutive films clear a
  floor I set.

**Being visible is not a topic change.** Putting Ahmed on camera does not
spend the off-band budget and does not blend the fit score. Presence is not a
subject. The outcome requires people to like Ahmed, and right now a stranger
scrolling the profile would say it is an account that posts beautiful sad
films, not that it is a filmmaker they follow. Fixing that is the highest
leverage move available and it costs nothing in reach.

### Expansion path

Each step unlocks on a trigger, not on a date.

1. **Craft visible inside the films.** Now. No trigger needed.
2. **Ahmed on camera and in voice.** Now. Not a topic change.
3. **Standalone craft breakdowns of my own films.** Unlocks when three of five
   consecutive films clear the reach floor I set.
4. **First product: a powergrade or LUT pack of the look people already ask
   about.** Unlocks when people ask how in the comments unprompted, three
   times. This is the right first product because it is the direct artifact of
   what is already praised, it needs no new skill to make, and it costs
   nothing to deliver.
5. **SFX or sound pack.** Unlocks once product one has sold at all.
6. **Tutorials, then a course.** Unlocks when the breakdowns hold engagement
   and there is a question I have been asked repeatedly and already answered
   in content.
7. **Gear partnerships.** Not a goal, a consequence. Unlocks when the craft
   association exists. Chasing it earlier means accepting the deals that
   damage the brand.

Return to the roots on purpose. Keep making films with no craft content
attached at all, and say that is what I am doing, so the people who came for
the feeling never feel sold to.

## A note on vulnerability

The standard creator advice is to share your failures. My audience is
literally men who read admitting failure as complaint, so confession does not
buy trust here the way it does in English-language content. Buy it with
specificity and cost instead: an exact year, an exact person, a decision that
was expensive.

Share the failure I have finished having. A failure with a conclusion is a
story. A failure still in progress is a request for support, and it turns
viewers into caretakers, which is not a brand.
